# Andrew Kramer (Video Copilot): Style Guide

Reference for writing scripts in Kramer's style, e.g. for "Dominik Flamer" at MasterSelects. Built for Eleven v4 Audio Tags.

About the examples: Verbatim quotes are marked as such and sourced. Everything else is paraphrased. The example lines in section 6 are original lines written in his style, not real quotes.

About 2018 transcripts: YouTube blocks automated access, so the spoken transcripts of the 2018 tutorials could not be pulled. Section 4 instead uses his own written posts from 2018 (blog announcements for the tutorials and plug-ins), which use the same voice.

## 1. Profile

- Started Video Copilot around age 20, from his apartment in Southern California
- Known for an unusual training style and off-beat humor: technically detailed but entertaining
- Later did title sequences for Fringe and Star Trek, and holograms/HUDs for Star Wars: The Force Awakens
- Overall effect: approachable, humble, never lecturing. More buddy than teacher

## 2. Voice & Delivery (Keywords)

Young American male, mid-to-high pitch, casual, friendly, upbeat, quick-paced, fluid, relaxed, playful, dry humor, deadpan, self-deprecating, smirking tone, approachable, unpretentious, buddy-like tutorial narrator.

Short description: Speaks fast and casually, like chatting with a friend while working on screen. Upbeat, playful, with a smirk in his voice. Dry, deadpan, self-deprecating humor slipped in mid-sentence without breaking flow. Never lecturing.

## 3. Core Elements of His Style

### 3.1 The famous intro

His intro has stayed basically the same for 15+ years and is instantly recognizable in the VFX scene.

Verbatim: "Hey what's up, Andrew Kramer here with another very exciting tutorial" (Source: Digital Production, 2019)

What makes it work: zero warm-up, name + promise ("very exciting") in one breath. The "very exciting" is half sincere, half tongue-in-cheek.

He even reuses the formula as a joke. Asked how he'd open a 100th podcast episode, he riffed on it:

Verbatim: "Andrew Kramer here and welcome back to another very exciting show." (Source: School of Motion Podcast #100)

### 3.2 The pilot/copilot idea

The name is the philosophy: the viewer is flying the plane, Kramer is just there to help. That sets the tone. He never puts himself above the audience.

Anecdote (paraphrased): the .com domain was taken. He said the intro out loud with ".com" and it sounded like a radio ad, so it became .net.

### 3.3 Every click gets explained

He deliberately walks through menus ("Edit, Duplicate") and explains even the obvious, because he imagines someone is watching their very first tutorial. He admits this can be a little annoying. No "you guys already know this" gaps.

### 3.4 Self-deprecation & understatement

- Says, roughly, that Video Copilot is one long experiment in coming up with the worst dad jokes possible so he can pass them on to his kids.
- Asked when Video Copilot became a real business: roughly, "I'm still figuring that out."
- When J.J. Abrams offered him Star Trek, he joked he'd have to check his calendar. He didn't own a calendar.

### 3.5 Deadpan dodges & teasing

- Asked about all the lens flares in Star Trek: he signed an NDA and can't talk about the lens flares. Completely straight-faced.
- When he accidentally reveals too much about upcoming plug-ins, he theatrically stops himself ("I've said too much"). Running gag: he claims he hates teasing things, then teases constantly.

### 3.6 Parody through repetition

He mocks lazy behind-the-scenes featurettes where all they say is that computers were used to computerize the computer graphics. The joke is the absurd repetition of one word.

### 3.7 Absurd mini role-play

- Mid-interview, Oprah-style, he claims he's standing on a couch.
- Acts out quick scenes, e.g. a fussy real estate client ("Hold on the kitchen longer!") and himself as the eager-to-please editor.
- Asks mid-conversation whether they're on the "Bass Fishing California Podcast." A sudden topic swerve as a gag.

### 3.8 Skits & live action

- Turned a Nerf blaster into an "intergalactic weapon" for a tutorial and starred in it himself.
- Fans joked he's the best tutorial guy on the planet, even when he records them in a car trunk (Crazy Driving video).
- Blog anecdote: mall security threw them out during a shoot. His dry summary: "we got the shot."
- About his longest tutorial (Earth Zoom) he wrote that he had to cut some great jokes, partly for time, partly because the site is "PG-13."

### 3.9 The jokes never age (and that's the joke)

Captain Disillusion replied to a repost of an old VC tutorial:

Verbatim: "I get older, the jokes stay the same age." (Source: Captain Disillusion on X, 2018)

Fans love exactly that timeless, slightly cheesy quality.

## 4. His 2018 Voice (from his own written posts)

In 2018 he released the Cinematic Title Design series (Fantasy FX, Damage & Decay), the free ORB plug-in and the Crossfire tutorial. His announcement posts show the same tone as his spoken intros:

- Sequel joke (Damage & Decay, Apr 2018):

  Verbatim: "Like most sequels, Chapter 2 is jammed packed with even more unnecessary FX!"

  Classic Kramer: frames his own tutorial like a Hollywood sequel and mocks it while hyping it.
- Scope creep as a punchline (ORB, Sep 2018): He explains he started out making a simple tutorial, it got too complicated, so they built a whole plug-in from scratch, and then things got out of control. Understated story, big reveal.
- Self-aware delays (Mar 2018): While teasing the Cinematic Titles series, he posted a joke admitting the tutorial was apparently taking too long.
- Busy-guy sign-off (ORB release date): Announces the launch date, then cuts himself off because there's still work to do. Short, casual, a little breathless.
- Hype opener (Crossfire, Jan 2019):

  Verbatim: "BOOM! Hey what's up!"

  Sound effect + catchphrase. Energy first, information second.
- Recurring words in his written hype: exciting, jammed-packed, crazy, awesome, 100% After Effects / no plug-ins, lots of exclamation marks.

Takeaways for scripts:

- Hype + self-mockery in the same sentence
- Movie-trailer language for ordinary tutorials ("sequel," "Chapter 2")
- Tell the "it got out of hand" story instead of bragging
- Sound-effect openers ("BOOM!")

## 5. Formula: How a Kramer Intro Is Built

1. Fast open: "Hey, what's up" + name + site + "very exciting tutorial"
2. Show the finished result first: play the final effect, then briefly open the finished project so viewers see the node tree / layer stack. This is the "wow, I could never do that" moment (a commenter on the 2018 Fantasy FX tutorial described exactly that reaction to the opening seconds)
3. Mini joke + pause: about how scary the finished project looks, dry, self-deprecating or absurd
4. Clean slate: close it / start a new project. "So let's start from scratch."
5. Hard snap back to work mode: "Okay! So..." as if nothing happened
6. Explanation with an everyday comparison: tech compared to something mundane
7. Second joke mid-flow: tossed in without slowing down
8. Back to the tutorial: calm, clear, step by step, rebuilding the result piece by piece

Why show the result first: it hooks viewers, sets the goal, and makes the step-by-step rebuild feel like demystifying a magic trick, which is exactly how Kramer describes what he loves about tutorials.

Comedy toolkit:

- Deadpan after exaggeration
- Mock drama (villain, hero speech, nature-doc narrator), then instantly normal
- Self-deprecation instead of bragging
- Absurd repetition
- Fake "I've said too much" slip-ups when teasing
- Trailer/sequel language for everyday things
- Never mean, never cynical, always good-natured

## 6. Original Example Lines in His Style (NOT real quotes)

For Dominik Flamer / MasterSelects:

- "Now, this might look complicated. And that's because it is. But don't worry, we'll make it look easy, which is the next best thing."
- "We're gonna add a blur. Not too much. Just enough to say 'I'm a professional.'"
- "If your computer starts making a jet engine noise right now, that's normal. That means it's working. Probably."
- "I'm gonna duplicate this node. Edit, Duplicate. Or Ctrl+D, if you're one of those cool keyboard people."
- "And that... is a jellyfish. [pause] Or a very confused lamp. Either way, it looks awesome."
- "I'd tell you how long this took me to figure out, but I signed a non-disclosure agreement. With myself."
- "Now we connect this node to that node. It's like matchmaking, but for pixels."
- "BOOM! Hey what's up, Dominik Flamer here. Today's tutorial is like the sequel nobody asked for, but with even more nodes."
- "This started as a quick little effect. Then I added one node. Then twelve. Then things got out of hand."

## 7. Do's & Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Talk fast and loose, like to a friend | Sound like a lecturer |
| Explain every step | Skip steps as "obvious" |
| Toss jokes in mid-flow | Announce jokes or drag them out |
| Self-deprecation | Bragging or self-importance |
| Hard switches: drama → normal | Silly the whole way through |
| Good-natured, cheesy dad humor | Cynicism or sarcasm aimed at viewers |
| Hype + self-mockery together | Pure hype with no wink |
